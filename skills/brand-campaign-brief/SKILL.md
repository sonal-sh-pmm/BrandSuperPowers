# Campaign Brief

A marketing counterpart to `superpowers:brainstorming`: find out what is actually wanted and why, then write it down before any asset is produced.

## Steps

1. **Ground the session.** Read `docs/brand/positioning-and-claims.md` and `docs/brand/terminology.md`. If they are stubs, say so; the brief can still be drafted but every message must be marked UNAPPROVED.
2. **Ask, one question at a time** (use multiple choice where possible): objective and how success is measured, audience and stage of buying, the single main message, offer and call to action, channels, timeline, budget range, owners and approvers.
3. **Propose 2-3 approaches** with a recommendation and trade-offs.
4. **Draft the brief in sections**, confirming each with the user.
5. **Save** to `docs/brand/briefs/YYYY-MM-DD-<campaign>.md` using the template below.
6. **Hand off.** List the assets needed and which skill produces each (`brand-copy-review`, `brand-decks-and-visuals`).

## Template

```markdown
# <Campaign name>
Status: draft | in review | approved   Owner: <NAME>   Date: <DATE>

## Objective and success metrics
<Metric, baseline, target, measurement source. State sample sizes and caveats for any evidence. No causal claims from correlation.>

## Audience
<Segment, role, stage. No customer-identifiable data.>

## Core message and proof
| Message | Status (CONFIRMED / ASPIRATIONAL / UNAPPROVED) | Evidence |
|---------|-----------------------------------------------|----------|

## Offer and call to action

## Channels and assets

## Timeline and owners

## Risks, assumptions, open questions

## Approvals required
<Legal, product marketing, brand, customer permissions>
```

## Don'ts

- Don't commit to pricing, dates, support levels or capabilities that are not confirmed.
- Don't reuse one customer's confidential information in another's materials.
- Don't call a brief approved; approval is recorded by a named person.
